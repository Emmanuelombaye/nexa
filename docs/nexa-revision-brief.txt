09  How It Works, Pricing, and FAQ
These pages should answer the operational questions that determine conversion and reduce support volume.
How It Works — replace general language with this sequence
1. Choose a care program — Select medical weight management, men’s hormone health, women’s hormone health, or peptide therapy.
2. Complete secure intake — Provide medical information inside the secure clinical portal—not on the public marketing site.
3. Meet a licensed clinician — The clinician reviews medical history, symptoms, labs when required, and treatment eligibility.
4. Receive a clinical decision — A prescription is issued only when medically appropriate. Treatment is not guaranteed.
5. Pharmacy fulfillment — When prescribed, the medication is dispensed by the applicable licensed pharmacy and shipped where legally available.
6. Ongoing care — Explain the exact follow-up, messaging, refill, and lab cadence included in the program.
Pricing page — required fields for every program
Charge due today.
Recurring amount and billing frequency.
Consultation fee and whether it is refundable if the patient is not eligible.
Medication cost and whether dosage changes the price.
Lab cost and frequency.
Supplies and shipping.
Follow-up visits, clinical messaging, and refill support.
Cancellation process, timing, and any non-refundable fees.
State, pharmacy, product, and eligibility limitations.

ARE THESE RESEARCH-USE PEPTIDES?
No. Nexa Rx does not sell research-use-only products. Peptide therapies offered through the clinical program require a medical evaluation and prescription and are dispensed for eligible patients through the applicable pharmacy channel.
\10  Technical, SEO, privacy, and conversion requirements
Fix these before the site moves from Vercel draft to production.
Priority
Current item
Change to
Why / team instruction
P0
URL structure uses /#/
Use clean History API routes and server-side rendering, static rendering, or hydration for public pages.
Google generally does not support URL fragments to change page content and recommends renderable, indexable JavaScript implementations.[7][10]
P0
One generic page shell/title
Create unique title tags, descriptions, canonical tags, headings, Open Graph data, and indexable copy for every program page.
Improves search visibility, ad landing-page relevance, and sharing previews.
P1
No visible sitemap or structured-data plan
Add XML sitemap, Organization markup, Breadcrumb markup, and accurate product markup for supplements where applicable.
Helps search engines understand the company and page hierarchy. Do not mark up content that is not visible.
P0
Health selections may be captured by marketing tools
Audit Meta, Google, analytics, session replay, forms, chat, and URL parameters. Do not send medical intake or identifiable health data to ad platforms.
HHS has specific guidance on online tracking technologies for regulated entities; the prudent design is to keep clinical data out of marketing tools.[8]
P0
Public forms may expand into health questions
Limit public lead forms to basic contact, state, and program interest. Move symptoms, diagnoses, medications, labs, and medical history into the clinical portal.
Reduces privacy exposure and keeps marketing systems out of the medical record flow.
P1
No explicit supplement/clinical account separation
Keep supplement orders in the commerce stack and clinical data in the patient portal. Use a carefully designed account handoff rather than one shared database by default.
Simplifies privacy, support, data governance, and vendor responsibility.
P1
Small gray text and very small top-bar copy
Increase contrast and minimum mobile text size; test all pages at common iPhone and Android widths.
The current design is elegant but some text is hard to read.
P1
No visible sticky mobile CTA plan
Add a mobile sticky “Check Eligibility” button on clinical pages and a “Shop Supplements” button only on shop pages.
Improves conversion without mixing the two customer journeys.
